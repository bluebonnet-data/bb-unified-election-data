The short version

From ten counties to the whole state

236 of 254 Texas counties now have U.S. House results (2016 to 2024) translated onto the 2026 congressional map. All of it is committed to the repo, and every county passes a vote leakage check.

When the pilot ended we had ten counties, done by hand. The work since has been about getting from ten to the whole state, and finding out what breaks along the way.

What we built

The machinery

A batch runner

One command now takes any county through the full pipeline: load its precincts, build the weights table against the 2026 districts, interpolate the votes, and check the result. Run it across the registry and it does all 254, writing a report on every one.

A precinct ID reconciler

This was the real unlock, and it is worth explaining, because it is where the time went.

Election results and precinct boundaries come from different sources, and they disagree about what a precinct is called. One county's results say 0050001 where its map says 50001. Another says 190007 where the map says 370007. Some IDs carry suffixes the map has never heard of. When an ID does not match, that precinct's votes have nowhere to land. They vanish. That is vote leakage, and at first it was affecting 47 counties.

The reconciler tries the ID as written, then a handful of known variants, and keeps whichever one actually matches a real precinct on the map. It never forces a match. If nothing fits, the county gets flagged rather than quietly given wrong data.

It took the review pile from 47 counties down to 8.

Leakage checking at scale

Every county, every cycle, we compare votes reported against votes after interpolation. Nothing gets saved to the dataset unless it passes. Counties that fail still run, so we can see why, but they write nothing.

What we learned

Three things that surprised us

Precinct ID mismatches were the whole ballgame

Not 254 unique problems, but a handful of patterns repeated across counties. Once we could name them, we could fix them in one place.

Uncontested races need to be handled honestly

When one party fields no candidate, the two party vote share is not zero. It is undefined. We mark those cycles rather than plotting a misleading number.

A county is not a district

Each county file holds that county's contribution to the districts that overlap it. Tarrant County touches seven districts, and some of them are barely in Tarrant at all. This is correct, but it is easy to misread, and it points at something bigger.

The honest question

Districts are a fine starting point

District level works. We can now put every cycle from 2016 to 2024 on the same map and see how a place moved. That was the goal, and it is done.

But a district is a big place. Knowing that one of Tarrant County's seven districts shifted a few points does not tell an organizer where to go on a Saturday. The question people actually have is about neighborhoods, and a district is too coarse a unit to answer it.

Districts are a fine starting point.
They are probably not good enough.

Precinct level is where the neighborhood view lives, and the method is identical: same interpolation, same leakage check, with a fixed precinct map as the target instead of a district map. The hard part is scale. Thousands of target shapes instead of thirty eight, across 236 counties.

We think the next real step is to pilot it on a county or two and find out whether it delivers what we want before committing to the whole state.
